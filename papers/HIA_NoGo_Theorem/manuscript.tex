\begin{abstract}
We report a historical numerical study of selected late-time ($z<0.5$) implementations considered during 25 iterations of the Holographic Information Alignment (HIA) program. The tested set includes a localized kinematic expansion modification, a screened-gravity route to a Type-Ia-supernova luminosity shift, and one smooth disformal photon-propagation implementation. Within the assumptions and parameterizations recorded here, the implementations encounter, respectively, acoustic-geometry/parameter-compensation tension, suppression in screened white-dwarf environments, and a photon--gravitational-wave arrival-time difference incompatible with the adopted GW170817 constraint. These results exclude the tested implementations or stated subclasses only. They do not constitute a theorem covering all smooth late-time theories, do not exhaust late-time solution space, and do not validate or falsify the Tech-Orb mother hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Tested Kinematic Bound}
In a flat $\Lambda$CDM-like background, the CMB angular scale is $\theta_* = r_s/D_A$. In the tested localized-gain parameterization, maintaining this scale while increasing local $H(z)$ at $z<0.5$ required compensating changes in $H_{\rm base}$. The stored joint optimizer, with its declared physical-density locking, returned values below $60$ km/s/Mpc for the target gain. This is a negative result for that implementation and its adopted constraints; it is not a proof about every late-time expansion model.

\section{Tested Screened-Gravity Implementation}
The study considered whether a local change in $G_{\rm eff}$ could shift Type-Ia-supernova luminosities. Under the adopted Chameleon/Symmetron-like screening assumptions, the white-dwarf interior was strongly screened and the recorded internal change was below that required by the proposed mechanism while Solar-System constraints were imposed. This bounds the tested implementation and assumptions, not all modified-gravity models.

\section{Tested Smooth Disformal Propagation Implementation}
The stored implementation modifies the photon metric using a smooth disformal coupling,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+D(\Phi)\partial_\mu\Phi\partial_\nu\Phi.
\end{equation}
For the parameter point chosen to produce the target distance-modulus shift, the recorded ray trace yielded an arrival-time advance of order $10^8$ seconds for a source distance associated with GW170817, far outside the adopted observational timing bound. This excludes that parameterized route as implemented. Full provenance and independent reproduction should be supplied before quantitative reuse.

\section{Conclusion}
The historical experiments identify useful failure modes for three tested late-time routes. They motivate stronger constraint checks and broader likelihood analysis, but they do not establish that late-time solution space is exhausted and do not imply that early-universe physics must be correct. Future claims must declare scope, reproduce the implementation, and separate pipeline validity from model survival.
